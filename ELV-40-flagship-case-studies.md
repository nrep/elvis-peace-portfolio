## 1. African-language AI / NLP

### Working title

**Making language technology useful for underrepresented African languages**

### One-line summary

At Digital Umuganda, Elvis works on language technology spanning NLP pipelines, speech recognition, and on-device models, bringing senior full-stack delivery to an area where language resources and deployment conditions are constrained.

### Problem

Many African languages remain underserved by mainstream language technology. Useful products must contend with limited language resources and practical deployment constraints while still providing dependable user experiences.

### Role

**Publicly supported:** Senior Full-Stack Engineer at Digital Umuganda; public profile identifies African-language technology, NLP pipelines, speech recognition, and on-device language models as areas of work.

**Confirm:** project name, language(s), dates, team size, exact ownership, stakeholders, and whether the work shipped to production.

### Constraints

- **Confirm:** dataset size/quality, annotation process, privacy or consent obligations, connectivity, device limits, accuracy targets, and multilingual edge cases.
- Do not claim a specific model, dataset, language, or benchmark until first-party evidence is available.

### Decisions

Use the case study's central decision only after confirmation. The strongest likely editorial angle is the trade-off between model capability and deployability: what ran on-device, what stayed server-side, and why. Record alternatives considered, evaluation criteria, and Elvis's decision authority.

### Outcome

**Current evidence:** the work area and Elvis's role are public; no defensible project-level outcome is yet available.

**Evidence needed:** accuracy/error-rate comparison, latency, supported languages, corpus or user scale, adoption, release status, or an approved qualitative result from a product owner.

### Visuals

1. Hero: approved product screen or language interaction in context.
2. Simple pipeline: input → preprocessing → model/service → product response.
3. Evidence panel: evaluation result, before/after example, or deployment constraint.
4. Optional: anonymized annotation or testing artifact.

### Links

## 2. Enterprise systems / microservices

### Working title

**Designing reliable systems around real operational workflows**

### One-line summary

Elvis's public profile describes production work across manufacturing execution systems, ERP platforms, enterprise management systems, and microservices—experience that can anchor a case study about turning complex operations into maintainable software.

### Problem

Operational teams need software that preserves workflow integrity across roles, services, and changing business rules. The portfolio needs one named system that demonstrates reliability and business impact rather than a generic architecture claim.

### Role

**Publicly supported:** senior full-stack experience and stated work across MES, ERP, enterprise systems, and microservices.

**Confirm:** employer/client, system name or approved anonymized label, dates, team, exact modules/services owned, design authority, and production support responsibilities.

### Constraints

- **Confirm:** legacy integrations, uptime or recovery needs, data consistency, permissions, audit requirements, intermittent connectivity, deployment environment, and confidentiality.
- If the system cannot be named, disclose industry, workflow, scale range, and Elvis's scope with written approval.

### Decisions

Center the story on one consequential system decision—for example, service boundaries, synchronization strategy, incremental replacement of legacy workflows, or observability. Show the rejected options and the operational reason for the final choice; avoid architecture diagrams that do not explain a trade-off.

### Outcome

**Current evidence:** enterprise and microservices capability is publicly claimed; no specific system result is publicly attributable.

**Evidence needed:** deployment frequency, processing time, incident/error reduction, workflow coverage, number of sites/users/services, reconciliation improvement, or an approved qualitative statement from the operating team.

### Visuals

1. Hero: anonymized operational dashboard or workflow screen.
2. Before/after workflow map focused on one operator task.
3. Small system-context diagram showing only relevant services and integrations.
4. Evidence panel with one verified operating metric or testimonial.

### Links

## 3. Founder-led product — Diolichat / Waguani

### Working title

**Helping Rwandan small businesses become easier to discover online**

### One-line summary

As Co-Founder and CTO of Diolichat, Elvis helped lead a software company focused on digital visibility and customer connections for small and startup businesses; its Waguani marketplace is publicly listed as an MVP with 50 active users.

### Problem

Small businesses and individual sellers need a practical way to present their offers and connect with consumers. Diolichat positioned Waguani as a centralized digital platform bridging that discovery gap.

### Role

**Publicly supported:** Co-Founder and CTO of Diolichat Ltd.

**Confirm:** dates, product strategy responsibility, technical architecture, code ownership, hiring/team leadership, customer discovery work, and launch responsibilities.

### Constraints

**Confirm:** founder budget and timeline, team size, target devices, connectivity/payment/discovery constraints, acquisition channel, moderation or trust needs, and how “active user” was defined.

### Decisions

The strongest case-study decision is likely how the team scoped a broad local-business vision into an MVP. Confirm the first target user, features deliberately excluded, technical stack, and the evidence used to prioritize marketplace discovery over adjacent services.

### Outcome

**Publicly supported:** Hanga Central lists Waguani as an MVP with 50 active users. The same profile lists two additional products: SARMS, a student assessment results management system, and eBiz, a web-based sales and inventory management system.

**Confirm before publishing:** reporting date and definition of the 50-user figure; whether Elvis approves using it; retention, transactions/listings, participating businesses, customer feedback, and the product's present status.

### Visuals

1. Hero: approved Waguani marketplace or seller-listing screen.
2. Founder decision log: initial opportunity → MVP scope → evidence → next iteration.
3. Compact product ecosystem view showing Waguani, SARMS, and eBiz only if it clarifies Diolichat's strategy.
4. Evidence panel with the verified active-user figure and date.
